# SAFE 1 — Subject Matter 

## 1.1 Research description
This project evaluates how migration over the last 10 years is associated with:
- childbirth-related outcomes, and
- population growth measures,
in Aotearoa New Zealand.

The work has two components:
1. **Evaluation (historical analysis):**  
   Assess relationships over time between migration conditions (using generic migration/cohort indicators available in approved datasets) and childbirth outcomes.

2. **Scenario forecasting (future planning):**  
   Produce forecasts under multiple plausible migration scenarios, combined with demographic structure effects.

## 1.2 Why linked microdata is needed
Migration effects on childbirth can depend on cohort and timing relationships, which may not be fully captured through aggregate-only data. Linked microdata (within Stats NZ Data Lab’s approved environment) enables:
- more defensible cohort/time-related analysis,
- improved alignment of numerators/denominators for rates and indicator construction,
- better-supported scenario forecasting inputs.

## 1.3 Public interest
The project is in the public interest because it can:
- support evidence-based planning and evaluation relating to population growth and service needs,
- provide insight relevant to government and public organisations when assessing impacts associated with migration,
- contribute to long-term demographic understanding and stakeholder discussions in New Zealand communities.

## 1.4 Responsible interpretation
Findings will be interpreted using **Ngā Tikanga Paihere** principles to support culturally safe and accountable communication, including:
- transparency about uncertainty,
- avoidance of deficit framing,
- responsible contextualisation of results and limitations.
